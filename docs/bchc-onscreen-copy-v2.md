### Part 3: the process on its own terms

New slide **14a `setup`** goes between 14 and 15.
- Title: "Before any building starts, agents load our setup."
- Story A: "Every project starts from the same setup: what agents learned before, what they're allowed to do, their skills, our modules, our design system and our tech stack."
- Story B: "Two orchestrator agents read all of it before they plan anything."
- Labels: "memory of past work" / "permissions" / "skills" / "modules" / "design system" / "tech stack and specs"
- Motion: the six inputs appear one at a time. Each one flows into two orchestrator agents at the centre, and the orchestrators light up.

Slides 15 to 17 now go into detail on three of those inputs.
- 15 Story A: "Everything they build starts from our design system, so every tool looks and feels the same."

Slide **19 `split`** is rebuilt in the style of the fleet film (`/architecture/control-room`, `src/components/control-room/`). It takes two clicks.
- Title: "Orchestrator agents break the work into epics, sprints and waves."
- Click 1, `plan`. Story A: "They turn the project into epics, sprints and waves of tasks, and a person approves the plan." The project row splits into workstreams (epics) of different sizes, divided into sprints, with a critical path through the dependencies. A coral approval follows.
- Click 2, `work`. Story B: "Then agents take the tasks among themselves: builders build, testers test, reviewers read the code and a fixer takes anything that fails." Agents arrive in waves and assign themselves to rows. Moving dots ride the cells as they fill, and the project row fills as tasks finish. Tests pass green.
- Slides 20 to 22 continue from this board.
